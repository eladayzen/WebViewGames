Four concept frames for Bloop Squad. Model: nano-banana-pro, 16:9.
All four share: strict overhead camera, wide landscape framing, pod low-centre
firing upward, monsters at the top AND at both edges AND below the pod (the
pass-the-player idea), candy + confetti, no text, no UI.

01 — night garden, three large monsters. Style anchor: warm picture-book,
     rim-lit characters glowing against a dark field.
02 — night garden, DENSITY TEST: twelve fist-sized monsters plus the bubble
     ribbon curving to trace where the pod has just been.
03 — candy world, glossy jelly monsters, plus the Mama monster: haloed, joined
     to two smaller monsters by golden ribbons of light (the healer tether).
04 — under the bed, patchwork sock monsters, knitted texture, torchlight.

Verdict recorded in ../brief.md: 02 is the target read, 03 owns the Mama
tether, 04 is charming but renders too literal and too 3D for the game.

--- second batch: two new worlds, two new rendering idioms ---
05 — bubble bath. Suds monsters, rubber ducks, warm rim of bathroom light.
06 — deep sea. Glowing jellyfish monsters, bioluminescence carrying the colour.
07 — SAME night-garden world as 02, redrawn in flat vector: heavy uniform
     outlines, no shading, limited palette. An idiom test, not a world test.
08 — claymation/felt: handmade clay monsters with googly eyes on a felt mat.
     Idiom test.

Read on the second batch (see brief): 06 is the strongest new world and the
best silhouettes of all eight; 07 proves flat vector reads at distance but goes
cold; 08 is the most charming and the least buildable — a clay look needs real
photographed frames or it dies in code. 05 is cute but its palette is pale
against a light tub, which is the opposite of what a dark field needs.

--- third batch: 2D, production-simple, closer to the reference, boys aged 8 ---
Brief for this batch: keep it flat 2D sprite art (bold dark outlines, two-tone
cel shading, saturated arcade palette, clean cuttable edges), black space
background like Viral Collapse, monsters with horns/fangs/one eye rather than
pastel sweetness, coins on death, pod low-centre firing up, monsters at every
edge including below the pod.

09 — twelve horned/fanged monsters, saucer pod, blue pellet stream, coins and
     confetti. The roster read.
10 — busy moment: three size tiers, a horned boss at the top, the curving
     pellet ribbon tracing the pod's path, a coin burst from a kill.
11 — limited palette + the Mama healer: crowned purple monster joined to two
     others by glowing beams. The tether read.
12 — five distinct monster TYPES in one frame (slime, spiky, fanged, eyestalk,
     armoured boss). The variety read.

Verdict recorded in ../brief.md: 09 is the art bible (silhouettes + palette),
10 is the gameplay frame (ribbon, tiers, coin burst), 11 owns the healer tether,
12 owns the type roster. All four are one visual family and buildable as sprites.

NOTE ON WHAT IS TRACKED. The repo ignores concept PNGs because they are large
and regenerable from the prompts above. That reasoning does not hold for the ONE
frame the art is locked to: image generation is not deterministic, so the prompt
is not a recipe for getting concept-09 back. A downscaled copy is therefore
committed as art-bible-concept-09.jpg (~200 KB) and is the reference every
sprite is matched against. The full-resolution PNGs stay local.
